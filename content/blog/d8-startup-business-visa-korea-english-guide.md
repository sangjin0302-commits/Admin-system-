The **D-8 visa** is Korea's primary route for foreign founders and corporate investors who want to build or run a business here. It is powerful but detail-sensitive: the difference between approval and refusal usually comes down to how the investment and business substance are documented.

> This is general guidance, not legal advice. Your eligibility depends on your specific facts and is confirmed after consultation.

## What the D-8 visa is for

D-8 is for foreign nationals who invest in, and actively manage, a Korean company. It is commonly used by:

- Founders incorporating a Korean startup
- Investors taking an equity and management role in a Korean company
- Corporate transferees establishing a local entity

## Core eligibility

While requirements are updated periodically, the D-8 assessment generally looks at:

1. **A validly incorporated Korean company** with proper business registration
2. **A qualifying investment** in that company from foreign-sourced funds
3. **A genuine, active management role** — not passive or nominal
4. **Real business substance** — office, plan, and operations consistent with the stated purpose

The investment amount and structure matter, and there are specialized D-8 sub-tracks (including technology/startup-oriented paths). Choosing the right sub-track is a key early decision.

## Documents that typically decide the case

- Certificate of business registration and corporate registry
- Evidence of the foreign investment (remittance records, capital verification)
- Shareholding and management documentation
- Business plan and proof of operations (office lease, contracts)
- Personal documents (passport, photos, forms) with certified translations

Immigration pays close attention to **where the money came from** and **whether you truly run the business**. Weak evidence on either point is the most frequent reason for refusal.

## Common reasons D-8 applications fail

- The investment cannot be clearly traced to foreign-sourced funds
- The company looks like a shell (no office, no real activity)
- The applicant's role appears nominal rather than managerial
- Document dates fall outside the accepted window or lack certified translation

## Timeline expectations

Incorporation, capital verification, and document certification take time and must happen in the right order. Starting document preparation early — before the filing window — prevents avoidable delays.
